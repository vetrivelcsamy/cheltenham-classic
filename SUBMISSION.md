## Blockers — only you can clear these

### 1. Look at the eszett

`ß` and `ẞ` are drawn and FontBakery passes, but they are drawn by stroking
a skeleton, not by eye. The weights come from each style's own stem and
hairline and they read correctly at text and display size, but the curves
are not the designer's. Worth a pass in Glyphs before submission, along with
the other glyphs in `src/DRAWING.md` section 2a.

### 2. Settle the family name before you submit, not after

Checked against the live catalogue and the naming rules, and this needs a
decision. It is cheap now and expensive later: a rename after submission
means the family name, the PostScript names, the filenames, `METADATA.pb`,
the repository, the deployed site and the release tags.

**No collision.** There is no `Cheltenham` in the 1,959 families Google
Fonts currently serves, and our OFL declares no Reserved Font Name, which is
what they require.

**But the naming rule is specific.** It says a family name must *"not include
registered trademarks"*, and separately: *"When creating a libre version of a
prior proprietary font or work in an established genre, add 'Libre' or a
local equivalent."*

Cheltenham is still sold commercially as ITC Cheltenham. That places it
nearer Franklin than Garamond — Garamond and Baskerville and Caslon name
long-dead punchcutters, and the catalogue uses them freely, but it reaches
for the qualifier where a living product shares the name:

| On Google Fonts | Historical name |
| --- | --- |
| `Libre Franklin` | ATF Franklin Gothic |
| `Libre Baskerville` | Baskerville |
| `Libre Caslon Text`, `Display`, `Condensed` | Caslon |
| `Libre Bodoni`, `Bodoni Moda` | Bodoni |
| `EB Garamond`, `Cormorant Garamond` | Garamond |

Every one carries a qualifier. `Cheltenham Classic` does too, so it may well
pass as it stands — "Classic" is doing the same work as "Libre" or "EB".

**What to do:** decide deliberately rather than discover it in review.
Either keep `Cheltenham Classic` and be ready to defend it, or rename to
`Libre Cheltenham` now, which matches their stated rule most directly and is
the form they have reached for four times already. I am not renaming it on
my own: it is your font's identity, your repository and your published site.

Whatever you choose, say in the submission issue that you checked, and why
you landed where you did. It costs a sentence and pre-empts the question.

### 3. Confirm the provenance, in writing

Google Fonts requires the design to be *"original, or a legitimate revival of
a design in the public domain."* They will ask, and the answer has to be
yours:

Cheltenham (Bertram Goodhue and Ingalls Kimball, 1896) is public domain, so
a revival is legitimate **in principle**. What the repository can show:

| Evidence | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| All six styles carry `Generated by: FontLab 8.0.0` | The outlines were produced in a font editor, not exported from another font's binary. |
| Source paths are your own folders — `C:/Users/vetri/Documents/Cheltenham-Modern/Cheltenham-Regular.vfc` and `C:/Users/vetri/OneDrive/Documents/Classic-Cheltenham/Classic Cheltenham {Medium,Bold,...}.vfc` | Local working files across two project folders, which is what original drawing over time looks like. |
| First commit 2024-08-09 | A year of history in the open. |

That is consistent with original work but does not prove it, and **only you
can answer the question they will actually ask**: were these outlines drawn
by you — from specimens, or from scratch — or does any of the geometry come
from an existing digital Cheltenham? If any does, that font's licence
governs and it has to be declared. Answer it in the issue before they ask.

One loose end while you are there: the `copyrights` property in every source
is a single space. `gf_fixup.py` writes the real string into the binaries, so
the shipped fonts are correct, but the sources should say it too.

### 5. Kerning by eye

`scripts/kern.py` measures the white between outlines and corrects pairs that
are loose against their own category's norm, which gets `AV`, `To`, `Yo`,
`LT` and `P.` to a reasonable place and leaves `nn`, `oo`, `HH` and body text
untouched. Accented letters now inherit their base's kerning through groups,
so `ÁV` closes up like `AV` and `Ł T` like `L T`.

It is a floor, not a substitute for kerning by eye. Numerals and the pairs
that only a designer notices are still untouched.

## Known warnings, and why they are left

Thirty remain, in five kinds. None is mechanically fixable from here.

| Warning | Why |
| --- | --- |
| `outline_jaggy_segments` | **Checked, and it is a false positive. Do not "fix" it.** The one glyph is `B`, at the waist node `(403, 383)` where the two bowls meet. Both control vectors point rightward, so the node is a deliberate corner — a reversal, not a broken smooth point — and the check flags the narrow angle between them because that *often* means an error. Rendered at 420px the waist is clean, with no kink. Smoothing the node to silence the check would flatten the waist and make the letter worse. |
| `vendor_id` | Now `VETR` rather than four spaces. It warns until the code is registered with Microsoft at <https://learn.microsoft.com/typography/vendors/>, which is your step. |
| `designer_profiles` | Clears once your profile is merged into `google/fonts`. |
| `contour_count` | The constructed glyphs differ from the shapes this check learned from. Resolves when they are redrawn. |
| `unreachable_subsetting` | The spacing accents and a few combining marks are not in any Google Fonts subset definition. Its suggestions — add `yi`, `old-permic`, `todhri` — are nonsense for a Latin face. Nothing to do. |

## What the metadata now carries

Compared against what Google Fonts actually ships for `Young Serif`,
`Libre Bodoni` and `Instrument Serif`, because their own entries are the
only reliable statement of the schema. Four things were missing:

| Added | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| `stroke: "SERIF"` | Separate from `category`; the catalogue filters on it |
| `commit` | Pins the exact upstream revision the submission was built from |
| `files { source_file, dest_file }` | Tells their tooling which binaries to take and what to call them |
| `config_yaml` | Points at `sources/config.yaml` |

`sources/config.yaml` is new. Google Fonts rebuilds families from upstream
with `gftools builder`, and without a config they cannot. It declares the six
sources, the family name, and static TTF/OTF/webfont output with no variable
axis, since each style is its own single-master source.

It is **not** what this repository builds with — `scripts/build.sh` is,
because the marks, the Latin Core expansion and the kerning are all
generated from measurements taken off a first build, which a declarative
config cannot express. The config exists so their pipeline can produce the
same six styles from the same sources.

## The submission itself

New families are submitted as an **issue**, not a pull request.

1. Open <https://github.com/google/fonts/issues/new?template=1_add-font.md>
   — the "Submitting a new OFL Font" template.
2. Title: `Add Cheltenham Classic`.
3. Point it at `https://github.com/vetrivelcsamy/cheltenham-classic`, which
   already has what they ask for: sources in `.glyphspackage` form, a
   `fontmake` build, FontBakery in CI, and an OFL.
4. Tag a release first so there is a fixed version to review.

Draft issue text is in `gf-submission/README.md`, including the
provenance paragraph you have to write yourself.
